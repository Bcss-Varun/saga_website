CAPABILITIES

What Governance teams get.
Constituency and district sentiment

Sentiment is resolved to the constituency and the district rather than reported as one national figure. An administration can see which areas moved on a scheme; a party office can see where its position is being tested. Each area reading opens back to the posts it was built from.

Unrest score, five levels

Every area carries a 0–100 score across five levels [pending sign-off: band labels]. The score is driven by issue velocity — the rate at which a topic is accelerating in that area — so a small district moving quickly outranks a large one that is merely loud. The issue driving the score is named alongside it.

Grievance routing and resolution

Complaints are categorised by subject and routed to the department that owns them, then tracked through New, In Progress and Resolved. Time in each state, the department holding it and who closed it are recorded, so resolution can be reported by department and by district instead of as one total.

Coordinated activity attribution

Activity is attributed as organic or coordinated from account age, posting cadence, repeated phrasing and burst timing [pending sign-off: attribution thresholds]. A set of accounts posting one line within a short window reads differently from the same volume of people reacting, and the two are reported separately rather than summed.

Event and announcement impact

Compare the same constituencies before and after an announcement, rally, budget or policy change. Impact is measured against each area’s own prior reading, so a district that started negative and improved is not hidden inside a national average that fell.

Daily briefing

A scheduled briefing assembles the areas that moved, the issue driving each, activity flagged as coordinated, and grievances still open past their expected resolution. It is built from the same records as the rest of the platform, so any line in it opens back to the posts and complaints beneath it.

One issue, followed from the post to the district to the department.

A sentiment shift in one constituency, the issue velocity behind it, the grievances filed against that issue and the department holding them are one record read at different depths. An administration uses it to see where delivery is failing; a party office uses it to see where a position is not landing. Neither has to take the other’s word for the underlying data.

SCENARIOS

What this looks like in practice.

Illustrative. Anonymised, with no real names or places.

A scheme lands differently in two districts

A newly announced scheme reads positively across the state as a whole. Resolved to district level, two adjacent districts move the other way, both on the same eligibility question. The unrest score rises in both on issue velocity rather than volume, and the grievances filed name the same criterion. The department responsible sees the pattern before it reaches a national outlet.

A hundred accounts, one sentence

A policy hashtag accelerates sharply overnight. Attribution separates the burst into two populations: accounts created within a short window posting near-identical phrasing, and a much smaller set of long-standing accounts writing in their own words. The coordinated portion is reported separately, so the genuine objection underneath it is neither dismissed nor inflated.

A backlog that shows up before the complaint does

Grievances on one subject in one district stay in In Progress well past the time they normally take to close. No individual complaint is unusual; the time-in-state pattern is. The briefing surfaces the department holding them and the district affected, while the issue is still administrative rather than public.
