# Blura SAGA vertical content and layout plan

Updated: 2026-09-14
Status: revised planning brief and draft copy; page layouts are not implemented yet.

## Direction

Each vertical is a complete first landing page for a visitor arriving from search, a shared link or a proposal. No prior visit to Home, Platform, Investigation or Trust is assumed.

The public product name is **Blura SAGA**. Public Safety, Governance, Brands and Celebrity are solution areas within that platform, not separate products.

This brief supersedes the identical nine-block vertical template in CLAUDE.md for the requested redesign. Retain the site's dark palette, orange headings, Outfit typography and accessibility conventions. Use shared components but compose pages around each audience's needs.

## What every visitor must understand

The page must answer these questions in order:
1. Is this relevant to my role and problem?
2. What is Blura SAGA, and what information does it work with?
3. Which capabilities address my needs, and how do they work?
4. What would my team do with the results?
5. What would an example workflow look like?
6. What are the relevant limits, access considerations and next steps?

A hero contains the full product name, audience, plain-language product category, a useful outcome, and two actions: Request a Demo and an anchor to the first explanatory section on that page.

Immediately after the hero, introduce the product in the vertical's language. Explain monitored public digital signals and configured sources, interpretation of those signals, and the resulting alerts, records or briefings. Do not imply coverage of all platforms or private conversations.

Each feature explanation includes the problem, what the feature does, what the team sees, and how that informs a decision. Relevant limitations stay beside the feature; FAQs provide further detail.

## Section counts and layout

Public Safety: 10 sections. Governance: 10 sections. Brands: 9 sections. Celebrity: 9 sections.

These counts are editorial groupings, not limits on feature coverage. Features can have several substantive subsections. Brands and Celebrity include access and deployment context in their team-output section, with further answers in FAQs.

Avoid repeated rows of identical text cards. Alternate split feature sections, large explanatory diagrams, comparison panels and narrative examples. Essential content stays visible without tabs or carousels. Mobile order is heading, explanation, corresponding visual, then supporting detail. Keep essential labels as HTML.

No new in-page navigation bar is proposed; the existing site removed that component. Hero secondary actions can link to section anchors.

## Public Safety — /public-safety

Audience: police leadership, intelligence teams, investigating officers and operational coordinators.
Search intent to address in natural language: social media intelligence for law enforcement, police intelligence tools, digital investigation, multilingual monitoring. These are editorial hypotheses, not researched keyword-volume findings.

Draft title: Social Media Intelligence for Law Enforcement | Blura SAGA
Draft description: Explore Blura SAGA for public safety: multilingual monitoring, prioritised alerts, digital investigation and case workflows with source context.

### 1. Hero
Eyebrow: Blura SAGA for Public Safety
H1: Social media intelligence for informed public-safety response.
Intro: Blura SAGA is a social media intelligence platform that helps law-enforcement teams monitor relevant public digital signals, review emerging risks and organise findings for investigation. It connects watchlist matches, source context and case activity so officers can assess what needs attention and decide the next action.
Secondary action: Explore public-safety capabilities → #overview.
Visual: a dedicated public-safety illustration with clear text space; no operational findings or invented product measurements.

### 2. What Blura SAGA brings to your team — #overview
Explain the move from fragmented posts and media to reviewable intelligence. Introduce watchlists, language and subject context, prioritised alerts and investigation records in one short sequence.
Draft lead: A watchlist match is a starting point. Blura SAGA retains the matched content and its context, helps officers examine the signal, and connects relevant findings to the investigation that needs them.
Explain source configuration locally, then link to Platform for the full processing sequence.

### 3. Monitor signals and prioritise attention — #monitoring
Feature subsections: multilingual watchlists; native-script, transliterated and mixed-script matching; the matched term and captured source; alert priority and routing by geography or subject.
Explain what the officer receives and why ownership matters. Retain documented language detail; confirm current support before publishing revised claims. Unconfirmed escalation timing and tier names stay in editorial review.
Layout: copy beside a watchlist → matched record → assigned alert diagram.

### 4. Investigate accounts and verify media — #investigate
Explain account and alias exploration, username candidates, image metadata and deepfake analysis. Include how findings attach to the relevant investigation.
State that a username match needs verification, location metadata may be absent, and media analysis can require human review.
Layout: two substantial feature rows with an account-relationship diagram and a media-review illustration.
Optional depth: Explore investigation tools and their limitations → /investigation#tools.

### 5. Keep findings and follow-up together — #casework
Explain person-of-interest records, FIR linkage, original media and capture context, and grievance routing through New, In Progress and Resolved.
Distinguish investigative case records from departmental complaint handling.
Layout: annotated record diagram plus a compact grievance workflow.

### 6. From detection to an officer's decision — #workflow
Walk through a fictional watchlist match: capture → priority review → assignment → investigation → recorded action.
Each step names the responsible activity and its output. Do not imply that an automated flag establishes wrongdoing or guarantees resolution.
Layout: desktop sequence, vertically stacked on mobile.

### 7. Public-safety examples — #scenarios
Three clearly illustrative stories: a regional-language watchlist match; suspicious video needing verification; an image whose available metadata changes the interpretation.
Each contains trigger, relevant capability and the information available for the officer's next decision. Avoid invented successful deployment outcomes.

### 8. Access, evidence handling and deployment — #deployment
Locally explain access scoped to responsibility, source and capture context, and recorded activity. Distinguish documented functionality from pending details about roles, retention and hosting.
Further detail: Review access and audit controls → /trust.

### 9. Questions from public-safety teams — #faq
Cover what the platform is; monitored sources and language configuration; alert ownership; username-match limits; missing image metadata; human review of media; case linkage; deployment/access requirements.

### 10. Relevant next step — #contact
Headline: Explore Blura SAGA for your department.
Copy: Walk through the watchlists, investigation records and response responsibilities relevant to your team's requirements.
Primary action: Request a Demo → /contact.

Eyebrow: Blura SAGA for Governance
H1: Understand public concerns. Coordinate an informed response.
Intro: Blura SAGA is a social media intelligence platform for understanding public conversations, emerging local issues and grievances. It helps governance teams examine sentiment by district or constituency, identify the issues behind changes and follow complaints through the department responsible for response.
Secondary action: See how governance intelligence works → #overview.
Visual: geographic and issue relationships; no invented district findings.

### 2. How Blura SAGA supports governance — #overview
Draft lead: A state-wide average can hide a local concern. Blura SAGA connects public conversations and configured grievance inputs with issue and geographic context, helping teams see where attention is needed and inspect the source material behind a finding.
Explain configured public sources, sentiment and issue analysis, geographic context and team outputs. Clarify that observed digital conversation is not a census of public opinion.

Optional depth: How signals become intelligence → /platform#idea.

### 3. Understand issues by area — #local-intelligence
Explain constituency/district sentiment, the themes driving changes, issue acceleration and geographic risk scoring. A reading must carry its area, period and supporting context.
Discuss available geographic information without promising precise locations for every post. Exact score ranges and band labels require a confirmed specification.
Layout: area comparison and an issue-detail panel.

### 4. Explain changes around policies and events — #impact
Explain comparison of the same area before and after an announcement or event, and the issues associated with movement.
Explain indicators of coordinated amplification alongside organic responses. Patterns support review; they do not prove account identity or political intent.
Layout: before/after composition with a separate amplification explanation.

### 5. Route grievances and follow progress — #grievances
Explain categorisation, owning department, New/In Progress/Resolved states, time in state, and department/district reporting.
Show what moves between teams and what remains on the record. Tracking resolution is distinct from guaranteeing it.
Layout: wide lifecycle diagram with concise detail under each step.

### 6. Turn local findings into a briefing — #briefings
Describe changed areas, the issue behind each change, supporting records and unresolved grievances.
Illustrate the reading sequence: identify an area → examine the issue → review sources → inspect departmental status → prepare the briefing.
Layout: an illustrative briefing with annotations, without fictitious performance claims.

### 7. Governance examples — #scenarios
Three illustrative stories: differing district responses to a scheme; a repeated service complaint; coordinated amplification alongside substantive criticism.
Explain how a departmental team uses the findings. A political-operation example, if retained, must be labelled and framed separately.

### 8. Responsibilities, access and deployment — #deployment
Explain geographic/subject responsibilities and access boundaries. Present deployment and retention as requirements to establish, without inventing hosting options.
Further detail: Explore access, audit and data handling → /trust.

### 9. Governance questions — #faq
Cover what governance intelligence means; source configuration; geographic interpretation; sentiment limitations; policy comparisons; grievance ownership; briefing contents; access boundaries.
Answer the main questions on this page even if fuller explanations exist elsewhere.

### 10. Relevant next step — #contact
Headline: Explore Blura SAGA for your governance requirements.
Copy: Review the areas, issues, grievance responsibilities and briefing needs your team works with.
Primary action: Request a Demo → /contact.

## Internal-link contract

Links are optional next steps, never prerequisites for understanding a capability. Use ordinary descriptive links, not automatic redirects.

| Destination | What must already be explained locally | Suggested anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | Public/configured inputs, relevant analysis and the vertical's outputs | How Blura SAGA turns signals into intelligence |
| Investigation | The relevant tool, its output and its key limitation | Explore investigation tools and their limitations |
| Trust | Relevant access boundary, source context and handling considerations | Review access and data handling |
| Contact | Why a demonstration matters for this team's workflow | Request a Demo |

Use a relevant section anchor only when it exists. Avoid links after every sentence and generic Learn more labels. Do not send visitors to another vertical to explain a core feature.
Platform and Investigation should link back to relevant vertical use cases where appropriate.

## Images and visual production

Target one hero illustration and two or three explanatory visuals per vertical. CSS/HTML diagrams can cover additional workflows without requiring another raster image.
Review existing public/images/public-safety.webp, governance.webp, brands.webp and celebrity.webp for suitability before reuse.
Do not present an illustration as a real product screenshot. The current brief does not permit public product UI; use annotated conceptual diagrams and illustrative narratives.
No capability explanation exists only inside an image. Keep visible labels selectable and accessible. Use text alternatives where an image adds meaning.
Final illustration prompts and crops follow the approved copy and rough layouts. Do not generate images before defining their informational purpose.
